If you've decided to try nail extensions, the next question is usually which type. Acrylic, gel, poly gel, builder in a bottle: the names can be confusing, and each feels different. Understanding **acrylic vs gel nails** (and poly gel) comes down to three things: how they feel, how strong they are and how they look.

This guide compares the main nail systems in plain language, explains the difference between extensions and overlays, and helps you choose the one that fits your lifestyle.

## Acrylic Nails

**Feel:** sturdy.
**Best for:** length and bold shapes.

Acrylic is a classic, sturdy system. It's popular when you want long nails or dramatic shapes like coffin or stiletto, because it holds length well. At Xing, acrylic extensions are finished with glossy gel polish.

**Choose acrylic if:**

- You want long nails or bold shapes
- You want a very sturdy set
- You use your hands a lot and want extra strength

## Poly Gel Nails

**Feel:** lightweight.
**Best for:** a natural feel with strength.

Poly gel is a hybrid of acrylic and gel. It's strong yet lightweight, with a comfortable, natural feel. Many people who find acrylic too heavy prefer poly gel.

**Choose poly gel if:**

- You want strength without a heavy feel
- You want nails that feel close to natural
- You're trying extensions for everyday wear

## UV Builder Hard Gel

**Feel:** glossy, slightly flexible.
**Best for:** a glossy, natural look.

Hard gel is cured under a lamp for a glossy, natural-looking finish. It has a little flexibility, which many people find comfortable.

**Choose hard gel if:**

- You want a glossy, natural-looking finish
- You prefer a slightly flexible feel
- You want a refined, elegant look

## Builder in a Bottle

**Feel:** smooth, natural.
**Best for:** strengthening natural nails.

Builder in a bottle is a brush-on builder gel with a smooth, refined finish. It's especially popular as an overlay to strengthen natural nails and help you grow them out, and it can also be used for extensions.

**Choose builder in a bottle if:**

- You want to strengthen your own nails
- You're growing your natural nails out
- You prefer a smooth, natural look

## Extensions vs Overlays

Every system can be used in two ways.

| | Extensions | Overlays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What they do** | Add length and shape beyond your natural nail | Strengthen your natural nail without adding length |
| **Best for** | Longer nails, dramatic shapes, bigger nail art | Weak or breaking nails, growing nails out |

**Choose extensions** if you want instant length and shape.
**Choose overlays** if you like your nail length but want stronger, more polished nails.

## How to Choose for Your Lifestyle

Think about how you use your hands day to day:

- **Hands-on work or lots of typing:** shorter lengths in a sturdy system, or an overlay
- **Want a natural look for the office:** poly gel, hard gel or builder in a bottle at a short-to-medium length
- **Love long, statement nails:** acrylic for length and bold shapes
- **Want to grow your own nails:** a builder in a bottle overlay
- **Trying extensions for a wedding or party:** temporary extensions are an easy start

Your nail technician will help you choose based on your lifestyle.

## Keeping Your Extensions Looking Good

Whichever system you choose:

- **Use your fingertips, not your nails, as tools**
- **Wear gloves** for cleaning and washing dishes
- **Apply cuticle oil daily**
- **Book a refill every 2–3 weeks** as your nails grow
- **If a nail lifts or breaks, book a repair.** Don't glue it at home
- **Always get extensions professionally removed**

## Common Questions

**Do extensions damage natural nails?** Professional application and removal help protect your natural nails. Avoid picking or peeling extensions off, and always book a professional removal.

**Can I switch systems later?** Yes. Your nail technician can remove your current set and apply a different system.

**Which is best for first-timers?** Many first-timers start with temporary extensions or poly gel for a lightweight, natural feel.

## Refills, Repairs and Removal

Whichever system you choose, extensions need a little upkeep:

- **Refills:** as your natural nails grow, a gap appears near the cuticle. A refill fills the gap and keeps your set looking fresh. Book one every 2–3 weeks.
- **Repairs:** if a nail lifts or breaks, book a repair. Don't glue it at home, as that can trap moisture and cause problems.
- **Removal:** always book a professional removal. Picking or peeling extensions off can damage your natural nails. At Xing, we offer careful removal for acrylic, hard poly gel, gel polish and temporary extensions, including extensions done elsewhere.

## Overlays for Natural Nails

If you don't need extra length, an overlay may be all you need. Overlays strengthen your natural nails, protect them from breaking and give a polished base for colour or nail art. Builder in a bottle is especially popular as an overlay for growing nails out, while acrylic overlays add extra strength for people who are hard on their hands.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Choosing very long nails for a hands-on lifestyle**
- **Skipping refills**, which can lead to lifting
- **Peeling off extensions** instead of booking removal
- **Ignoring cuticle care**

## Extensions for Weddings and Events

If you're getting extensions for a wedding or a big event, book them a few days ahead so you can get used to the length, then add nail art closer to the day. For one-off occasions, temporary extensions are a quick, affordable option.
