Rough, dry heels are one of the most common foot complaints, and in a city where many people wear sandals and spend long hours on their feet, they're everywhere. At best they look untidy; at worst they crack, catch on socks and feel uncomfortable. The good news is that most rough heels respond well to simple, regular care. Good **cracked heels care** combines a daily home routine with regular pedicures and targeted treatments.

This guide explains why heels get rough, a home routine that helps, how pedicures and our Heel Peel Treatment work, and when to see a doctor.

## Why Do Heels Get Rough and Cracked?

The skin on your heels is naturally thicker than elsewhere, because it carries your weight. When it gets dry, it loses flexibility and can split under pressure. Common causes:

- **Dry skin**, especially in dry weather
- **Open footwear**, like sandals and slippers, which exposes heels to dust and lets the heel pad spread
- **Standing or walking for long hours**
- **Not moisturising** the feet
- **Very hot water** and harsh soaps
- **Hard floors** and walking barefoot often

For most people, it's a combination of dryness and pressure.

## When to See a Doctor First

Before trying any treatment, see a doctor if:

- **You have diabetes or circulation issues.** Foot care needs extra caution, and a doctor should guide it.
- **Cracks are deep, painful or bleeding**
- **You notice signs of infection**, such as redness, swelling, warmth or discharge
- **Rough skin doesn't improve** with regular care

Salon treatments help with dry, rough heels. They don't replace medical care.

## A Home Routine for Cracked Heels Care

Consistency matters more than any single product.

### Moisturise every day

- Apply a rich foot cream or moisturiser to your heels every day
- Night is the best time: moisturise, then wear soft cotton socks to help it absorb
- Don't moisturise between your toes, where skin needs to stay dry

### Exfoliate gently

- A few times a week, gently exfoliate softened heels with a foot file or pumice stone
- Soak your feet in lukewarm water first to soften the skin
- Use light pressure; the goal is smoothing, not removing everything in one go

### Avoid aggressive tools

Don't cut, shave or scrape hard skin aggressively at home. It can cause cuts, soreness and infection, and often makes skin grow back thicker.

### Choose footwear wisely

- Shoes with a closed back support the heel pad
- Alternate between sandals and closed shoes if you wear sandals often
- Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors for long periods

### Use lukewarm water

Very hot water dries skin out. Use lukewarm water for soaking and bathing.

## Cracked Heels Care Through the Seasons

- **Summer:** sandals and dust dry heels out. Moisturise daily and wash feet in the evening.
- **Monsoon:** damp feet can soften skin, but wet footwear and dirt add up. Dry your feet properly and keep up pedicures.
- **Winter:** drier air means drier skin. Use a richer foot cream and socks at night.

## Common Mistakes

- **Cutting or shaving hard skin at home**
- **Filing dry skin hard**, which can cause soreness
- **Skipping moisturiser** once heels look better
- **Ignoring pain or bleeding**
- **Soaking in very hot water**

## Heel Care for People on Their Feet All Day

If your work keeps you on your feet, your heels take extra pressure every day:

- **Choose supportive, cushioned footwear** with a closed back
- **Rest your feet** when you can, and put them up in the evening
- **Moisturise every night** without fail
- **Book pedicures regularly,** and add a Foot Massage for tired feet

## What to Expect After a Heel Peel Treatment

## Choosing a Foot Cream

Look for a thick, rich foot cream rather than a light body lotion, and apply it generously to your heels at night. If your skin is sensitive, choose a simple, fragrance-free option. Consistency matters far more than any particular product, so pick one you'll actually use every evening.
