Greys are a natural part of life, and how you handle them is entirely your choice. If you'd like to cover them, there are several ways to do it, each with a different look and level of upkeep. Understanding your **grey hair coverage** options helps you choose the one that suits your hair, your lifestyle and how often you want to visit the salon.

This guide compares global colour, root touch-ups, ammonia-free options and blending techniques, for both women and men.

## The Main Grey Hair Coverage Options

At Xing Premium Unisex Salon, the main ways to cover greys are:

1. **Global colour:** one even shade from roots to ends
2. **Root touch-up:** colour only on the new growth
3. **Ammonia-free versions** of both
4. **Highlights to blend greys** for a softer look

## Global Colour

**What it is:** one even shade applied from roots to ends.

**Best for:**

- Covering greys across your whole head
- First-time colour
- A full colour change
- A uniform, polished finish

**Grow-out:** a visible root line as your hair grows, so you'll need root touch-ups to maintain it.

## Root Touch-Up

**What it is:** colour applied only to the new growth at the roots, matched to your existing shade.

**Best for:**

- Keeping greys covered between global colours
- Maintaining your shade without recolouring your lengths

**How often:** most people book one every 4–6 weeks. Our guide to [how often you need a root touch-up](/blog/root-touch-up-frequency) explains what affects the timing.

## Global Colour vs Root Touch-Up

| | Global Colour | Root Touch-Up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What's coloured** | All hair, roots to ends | New growth only |
| **Best for** | Full grey coverage, first-time colour, a colour change | Maintaining coverage between global colours |
| **Effect on lengths** | Lengths are coloured again | Lengths are left alone |
| **Upkeep** | Root touch-ups as hair grows | Every 4–6 weeks for most people |

Most people use both: a global colour to start, then regular root touch-ups, with an occasional global colour when the lengths look faded.

## Ammonia-Free Options

Both global colour and root touch-up are available in ammonia-free versions at Xing. They give full, even colour with an ammonia-free formula, and are a popular choice if you colour often and prefer a gentler option. Read our guide to [ammonia-free hair colour](/blog/ammonia-free-hair-colour) to understand the pros and limits.

If it's your first time colouring or you have sensitive skin, ask about a patch test before your appointment.

## Blending Greys With Highlights

Not everyone wants full coverage. If you have some greys and want a softer, lower-maintenance look, highlights can help blend them. Lighter strands make regrowth less obvious, so greys growing in look more natural.

**Choose blending if:**

- You have a moderate number of greys
- You want a softer grow-out
- You'd like fewer salon visits
- You want a natural, multi-tonal look

Your stylist can tell you whether blending suits the number and placement of your greys.

## Choosing the Right Shade for Grey Coverage

The shade makes a big difference to how coverage looks and grows out:

- **A shade close to your natural base** usually looks the most natural and grows out more softly.
- **Very dark shades** can make regrowth show sharply against grey roots.
- **Warm or cool tones** should suit your skin undertone. Our guide to [hair colour for Indian skin](/blog/hair-colour-indian-skin-tone) explains how to choose.

## Grey Hair Coverage for Men

Men's grey coverage follows the same principles, with a few differences.

### Hair

- **Global colour** evens out greys all over
- **Ammonia-free global colour** offers a gentler formula
- **Highlights** can blend greys for a natural, textured look

Short haircuts often show regrowth faster, because there's less length to hide it, so regular upkeep keeps coverage looking natural.

### Beard

Greys often appear in the beard first. **Beard colour** evens out greys or patchy tones for a fuller-looking beard, and can be matched to your hair colour.

## Keeping Grey Coverage Looking Fresh

Fading colour makes greys show sooner. To keep coverage fresh:

- Use a colour-safe, sulphate-free shampoo
- Wash with cool or lukewarm water
- Protect your hair from strong sun
- Use a heat protectant before styling
- Book root touch-ups before regrowth becomes obvious
- Add a hair spa or Olaplex to keep coloured hair healthy

Our guide on [how to make hair colour last longer](/blog/make-hair-colour-last) has more tips.

## Planning Your Grey Coverage Year

A simple rhythm that works for many people:

| When | What to book |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Global colour, to even out greys all over |
| Every 4–6 weeks | Root touch-up, regular or ammonia-free |
| Every few months | Global colour again if your lengths look faded |
| Every few weeks | A hair spa to keep coloured hair soft |

Your own rhythm depends on how fast your hair grows and how visible your greys are. Your stylist will help you find it.

## Signs Your Grey Coverage Needs Attention

- A line of grey at your parting or hairline
- Greys showing at the temples, often the first place they appear
- Coloured lengths that look dull or faded compared with fresh roots
- Coverage that looks flat or slightly different in tone

Booking just before these become obvious keeps your colour looking natural.

## Common Grey Coverage Mistakes

- **Choosing a shade much darker than your natural colour,** which makes grey regrowth stand out
- **Recolouring the lengths every time,** which can dry them out
- **Waiting too long between touch-ups**
- **Mixing home colour and salon colour** without telling your stylist

## Grey Coverage and Your Haircut

Your cut affects how quickly greys show. Centre partings and sleek, pulled-back styles reveal regrowth sooner, while side partings and soft layers hide it a little longer. If you want to stretch the time between touch-ups, ask your stylist whether a small change in your parting or cut would help. Pairing your colour appointment with a haircut is also a good way to keep both looking fresh at the same time.
