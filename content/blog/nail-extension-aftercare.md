Nail extensions give you instant length, shape and a polished look. How long they keep looking that good depends largely on what happens between appointments. Good **nail extension aftercare** is simple, takes a few seconds a day, and protects both your extensions and the natural nails underneath.

This guide covers everyday habits, cuticle care, refills, what to do when something goes wrong, and why professional removal matters.

## Why Nail Extension Aftercare Matters

Extensions are strong, but they're attached to your natural nails. How you treat them affects:

- **How long they look fresh**
- **Whether they lift, crack or break**
- **How healthy your natural nails are** when the extensions come off

A few simple habits make all three much better.

## Everyday Habits

### Use your fingertips, not your nails

The most common cause of a broken extension is using nails as tools. Avoid:

- Opening cans and tins with your nails
- Peeling stickers or labels
- Scraping things off surfaces
- Pressing buttons with the tip of your nail

Use the pads of your fingers instead.

### Wear gloves for cleaning and dishes

Long soaks in water and contact with cleaning products can weaken extensions and cause lifting. Wear rubber gloves when washing dishes, cleaning or doing laundry by hand.

### Be careful with water

Brief contact with water is fine, but long soaks can affect how well extensions stay attached. Dry your hands properly after washing.

### Adjust gradually to length

If you've gone longer than usual, give yourself a few days to get used to typing, picking things up and doing up buttons.

## Cuticle Care

Healthy cuticles keep your nails looking neat and help your extensions look their best.

- **Apply cuticle oil daily.** It keeps the skin around your nails soft and moisturised.
- **Moisturise your hands** after washing.
- **Don't cut or bite your cuticles.** Leave cuticle care to your technician.

## Refills: Keeping Extensions Fresh

As your natural nails grow, a gap appears between the extension and your cuticle. A refill fills that gap.

**Why refills matter:**

- They keep your nails looking freshly done
- They keep the extension balanced, so it's less likely to lift or break
- They're quicker than a new full set

**How often:** book a refill every **2–3 weeks** as your nails grow. Refilling is available for extensions and overlays at Xing.

## When Something Goes Wrong

### A nail lifts

If an extension starts lifting at the edge or near the cuticle:

- **Book a repair** as soon as you can
- **Don't pick at it** or try to pull it off
- **Don't glue it at home.** Glue can trap moisture under the extension

### A nail cracks or breaks

- **Book a repair**
- If a broken edge is sharp, gently file it smooth until your appointment
- Avoid the temptation to remove the rest yourself

### Your natural nail feels sore

If the nail bed feels sore, or you notice redness or swelling around the nail, see your technician. If you have signs of an infection, consult a doctor.

## Safe Removal: Always Go Professional

This is one of the most important parts of nail extension aftercare.

**Never pick or peel extensions off.** Pulling them off can take layers of your natural nail with them, leaving nails thin, weak and sore.

Professional removal:

- **Protects your natural nails**
- **Uses the right method for your system**
- **Leaves your nails ready** for a new set, an overlay or a rest

## Giving Your Nails a Break

Some people like to give their natural nails a short break between sets. If you do:

- Keep nails short and filed while they recover
- Apply cuticle oil daily
- Consider a builder in a bottle overlay to strengthen natural nails while they grow
- Book a [manicure](/services/manicure) to keep nails and cuticles neat

## Aftercare for Different Systems

The basics are the same for every system, with a few small differences:

| System | Good to know |
| --- | --- |
| Acrylic | Very sturdy, but still needs refills and professional removal |
| Poly gel | Lightweight; avoid using nails as tools, as with any system |
| UV hard gel | Glossy finish; refills keep it balanced as nails grow |
| Builder in a bottle | Often used as an overlay; great for growing nails out |
| Temporary extensions | Short-term; book a removal when you're done |

To compare systems, read [acrylic vs gel vs poly gel](/blog/acrylic-vs-gel-vs-polygel).

## Nail Art on Extensions

Extensions give you more space for detailed designs. If your nail art is on gel polish, don't peel it off; book a professional removal. For design ideas, read [nail art ideas for every occasion](/blog/nail-art-ideas-occasions).

## A Daily Nail Extension Aftercare Checklist

- Cuticle oil
- Hand cream after washing
- Gloves for cleaning and dishes
- Fingertips, not nails, as tools
- Check for lifting at the edges

## A Note on Nail and Skin Infections

If you have a nail or skin infection, let us know before your appointment. Extensions shouldn't be applied over an infection, and if you notice signs of one with your extensions on, consult a doctor.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping refills**, which can lead to lifting and breakage
- **Gluing a lifted nail at home**
- **Peeling off extensions or gel polish**
- **Not wearing gloves** for cleaning and dishes
- **Ignoring cuticle care**

## Aftercare in the First 48 Hours

New extensions need a little extra care at first:

- Avoid very hot water and long soaks
- Be gentle while you get used to the length
- Avoid harsh cleaning products, or wear gloves
- Apply cuticle oil around, not on top of, the extensions

## Nail Extension Aftercare When Travelling

If you're travelling soon after your appointment:

- Pack a small cuticle oil and a soft nail file
- Avoid using your nails to open luggage or packaging
- Plan your refill for when you're back, about 2–3 weeks after application
- If a nail lifts on holiday, keep it clean and dry, and book a repair when you return; don't glue it

## Temporary Extensions

Temporary extensions are an easy option for an event or a first try. When you're done with them, book a professional removal, as with any extension.

## Signs You Need a Refill or Repair

- A visible gap between the extension and your cuticle
- An edge that's starting to lift or catch on fabric
- A crack, chip or a nail that feels loose
- Extensions that feel heavy or unbalanced as your nails grow

Book a refill or repair as soon as you notice any of these. Fixing small problems early keeps your set looking good and protects your natural nails underneath.

## Choosing the Right Length for Your Lifestyle

The easiest aftercare starts with choosing a sensible length. If you type a lot, cook, play sport or work with your hands, shorter extensions are less likely to snag, lift or break. You can always go longer for a special occasion. Your technician will help you choose a length and shape that suits your daily routine.
