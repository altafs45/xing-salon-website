Seeing more hair than usual in your comb, on your pillow or in the shower drain is worrying. Many people's first instinct is to book a salon treatment, and salons can genuinely help with some types of hair fall. But it's just as important to know what a salon can't do. This honest guide to **hair fall treatment salon** care explains the difference between breakage and hair loss, what a salon can help with, and when you need a dermatologist instead.

## First: Is It Breakage or Hair Loss?

These two look similar in your comb, but they're very different.

### Breakage

- Hair **snaps somewhere along its length**
- You see **short, broken pieces** of different lengths
- Ends look thin, frayed or split
- Often caused by damage: heat, colouring, rough handling, tight hairstyles, dryness

### Hair loss

- Hair **falls out from the root**
- The fallen hair is usually full length, often with a **small white bulb** at the root end
- You may notice a wider parting, thinning at the crown or patches
- Can have many causes, including medical ones

**How to check:** look at a few fallen hairs. Short pieces without a bulb suggest breakage. Full-length hairs with a bulb suggest shedding from the root.

## What a Salon Can't Do

Being honest about this matters:

- **A salon can't diagnose the cause of hair loss.** Hair loss from the root can be linked to health, hormones, nutrition, stress, medication and more.
- **A salon can't treat medical hair loss.** Conditions affecting the scalp or hair growth need a dermatologist.
- **No spa "stops" hair fall.** Spas can help reduce breakage and keep hair and scalp healthy, but they aren't a medical treatment.

If the issue persists, consult a dermatologist.

## Lifestyle Factors That Affect Hair

Some everyday factors are linked to hair health. A salon can't fix these, but it helps to be aware of them:

- **Stress** and poor sleep
- **Diet**, including whether you're eating enough protein and a balanced range of foods
- **Sudden weight changes** or crash dieting
- **Illness** or recovery from illness
- **Hormonal changes**, such as after pregnancy
- **Some medications**

If you think any of these may be involved, talk to a doctor or dermatologist.

## Everyday Habits That Reduce Breakage

These habits help reduce breakage, whatever the cause of your hair fall:

- **Detangle gently** with a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends
- **Avoid brushing wet hair roughly**
- **Use soft, fabric ties** instead of tight elastics
- **Avoid very tight hairstyles** that pull at the roots
- **Use a heat protectant** and moderate heat
- **Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase**
- **Use a gentle, sulphate-free shampoo** and condition the lengths
- **Get regular trims** to remove split ends

## When to See a Dermatologist

Consult a dermatologist if:

- Hair fall continues for weeks or keeps increasing
- You notice thinning, a widening parting or bald patches
- Your scalp is red, sore, scaly or inflamed
- Hair fall started suddenly after illness, a new medication or a big life change
- You're worried, for any reason

There's no harm in checking. Getting the right advice early is always better.

## A Sensible Plan

1. **Check:** is it breakage or hair loss?
2. **If breakage:** gentler habits, regular trims and strengthening hair spas
3. **If hair loss, thinning or patches:** consult a dermatologist
4. **Either way:** look after your scalp and handle your hair gently

## Hair Fall After Colour, Heat or Smoothing Treatments

Chemical services and heat can weaken hair, which can lead to breakage. If you've noticed more broken hair after colouring, lightening or a lot of heat styling:

- Tell your stylist what services you've had and when
- Consider a strengthening spa, such as Tribond or an Olaplex Hair Spa
- Use a heat protectant and lower heat on your tools
- Leave time between chemical services, as your stylist advises

## Seasonal Hair Fall

Many people notice their hair seems to shed more at certain times of the year. Seasonal changes, weather and stress can all play a part. If you notice a short-term increase that settles on its own, it's often not a cause for concern. If it continues or worries you, see a dermatologist.

## Questions to Ask at Your Consultation

- Does this look like breakage or hair loss?
- Which spa would help my hair and scalp?
- How many sittings would you recommend?
- Should I see a dermatologist?

A good stylist will answer honestly, including telling you when something is beyond what a salon can help with.

## Gentle Styling for Fragile Hair

If your hair is breaking, choose styles that don't pull: loose braids, low buns and soft ponytails with fabric ties. Avoid tight top knots, heavy clips and pulling hair back tightly every day. Give your hair heat-free days, and let it air-dry when you can. These small changes reduce stress on the hair and often make a noticeable difference to breakage within a few weeks.
