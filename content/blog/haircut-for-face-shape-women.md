Two people can bring the same reference photo to a salon and walk out with haircuts that look completely different on them. Face shape is a big reason why. The right **haircut for face shape** works with your features, balancing proportions and highlighting what you love about your face.

This guide shows you how to identify your face shape, which cuts tend to flatter each one, and how to use reference photos so your stylist can tailor the look to you.

## How to Find Your Face Shape

You'll need a mirror and good light.

1. **Pull your hair back** off your face.
2. **Look at the widest part of your face:** is it your forehead, cheekbones or jaw?
3. **Look at your jawline:** is it rounded, square, or pointed at the chin?
4. **Compare length and width:** is your face about as long as it is wide, or noticeably longer?

Then compare with these descriptions:

| Face shape | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Round | Similar length and width; soft, rounded jaw; full cheeks |
| Oval | Slightly longer than wide; gently rounded jaw; balanced proportions |
| Square | Similar length and width; strong, angular jaw; straight sides |
| Heart-shaped | Wider forehead and cheekbones; narrower, pointed chin |
| Long | Noticeably longer than wide; forehead, cheeks and jaw similar width |

Many people are a mix of two shapes. That's normal; your stylist will look at your face as a whole.

## Haircut for Face Shape: Round

**Goal:** add length and height to balance width.

**Often flattering:**

- Long layers that start below the chin
- A long bob (lob) that falls past the jaw
- Side partings and side-swept fringes
- Volume at the crown

**Use with care:** very round, chin-length bobs and heavy blunt fringes can make the face look rounder.

## Haircut for Face Shape: Oval

**Goal:** keep the natural balance.

**Often flattering:** almost everything, from pixie cuts to long layers, bobs, fringes and waves.

**Tip:** an oval face is a great canvas for trying new styles. Choose based on your hair texture and lifestyle.

## Haircut for Face Shape: Square

**Goal:** soften a strong jawline.

**Often flattering:**

- Soft, face-framing layers
- Waves and curls around the face
- Side-swept or wispy fringes
- Lengths that fall below the jaw

**Use with care:** blunt, jaw-length bobs and very straight, heavy fringes can emphasise the jaw.

## Haircut for Face Shape: Heart-Shaped

**Goal:** balance a wider forehead with volume lower down.

**Often flattering:**

- Chin-length or longer bobs with volume at the jaw
- Side-swept fringes to soften the forehead
- Layers that start around the chin
- Waves at the ends

**Use with care:** lots of volume at the crown can make the forehead look wider.

## Haircut for Face Shape: Long

**Goal:** add width and shorten the face visually.

**Often flattering:**

- Fringes, which shorten the face
- Layers at cheek level, which add width
- Bobs and lobs with volume at the sides
- Waves and curls

**Use with care:** very long, straight, one-length hair can make the face look longer.

## Face Shape Is Only Part of the Story

Your face shape is a useful guide, but it isn't the only thing that matters. Your stylist will also consider:

- **Hair texture:** fine, thick, straight, wavy or curly hair behaves very differently
- **Hair density:** how much hair you have
- **Your lifestyle:** how long you spend styling, and whether you tie your hair up for work or exercise
- **Your features:** your eyes, cheekbones, neck and even your eyebrows
- **What you like:** the most flattering cut is one you feel good wearing

## Using Reference Photos Well

Reference photos are one of the best ways to show what you want.

- **Bring two or three photos** of cuts you like
- **Choose photos on similar hair** in texture and thickness, if you can
- **Bring a photo of what you don't want** too
- **Explain what you like** about each photo: the length, the layers, the fringe?
- **Be open to adaptation.** Your stylist will tell you how the look will work with your hair texture and face shape, and adapt it if needed

Three tools stylists use to flatter every face shape:

- **Face-framing pieces** draw attention to your eyes and cheekbones on almost any face.

## Haircut Ideas by Hair Texture

Face shape tells you about proportions; hair texture tells you what's practical.

### Fine hair

Fine hair can look flat. Blunt ends and a few long layers keep it looking fuller. Bobs and lobs often suit fine hair well, because the shorter length makes it look thicker.

### Thick hair

Thick hair benefits from layers that remove weight, so it moves and doesn't look heavy. Long layers or a layered lob work for most face shapes.

### Wavy and curly hair

Waves and curls add width naturally, so take that into account. A round face might want curls to sit below the chin, while a long face can enjoy volume at the sides.

### Straight hair

Straight hair shows a sharp line beautifully, so blunt bobs and sleek cuts look striking. Layers or face-framing pieces add movement if it feels flat.

## Changing Your Look Gradually

If you want a big change but feel nervous, change gradually:

1. **Start with face-framing pieces** to see how shorter hair suits you
2. **Add layers** at your next appointment
3. **Then go shorter,** once you know what you like

Your stylist can plan a step-by-step change that keeps you comfortable.

## Questions to Ask Your Stylist

- What face shape do you think I have?
- Which of my reference photos will work best with my hair?
- How much styling will this cut need?
- How will it grow out?

## Haircuts for Glasses Wearers

If you wear glasses, think about how your frames sit with your cut. Face-framing pieces and fringes should work around your frames rather than hide them, and bold frames often pair well with simpler, sleeker cuts. Wear your glasses to your consultation so your stylist can see the full picture.
