White flakes on your shoulders, an itchy scalp, and the constant urge to scratch: dandruff is common, and it can be frustrating. Many people try one shampoo after another without understanding what's causing their flakes. Effective **dandruff treatment** starts with knowing the likely cause, then building a simple scalp routine and getting professional help when you need it.

This guide covers the common causes of dandruff and a flaky scalp, a home routine that helps, how a salon anti-dandruff hair spa can help, and when to see a dermatologist.

## Dandruff or a Dry Scalp?

They can look similar, but they're not quite the same.

| | Dry scalp | Dandruff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Flakes** | Small, white and dry | Often larger, can look yellowish or oily |
| **Scalp feel** | Tight, dry | Can feel oily and itchy |
| **Common triggers** | Hot water, harsh shampoos, dry air | Oily scalp, build-up, stress, weather changes |

Knowing which you have helps you choose the right care. If you're not sure, a dermatologist can tell you.

## Common Causes of a Flaky Scalp

Several everyday factors can contribute:

- **An oily scalp:** oil can build up and contribute to flaking.
- **A dry scalp:** hot water, harsh shampoos and dry air can leave the scalp tight and flaky.
- **Product build-up:** leftover shampoo, conditioner, gel or oil on the scalp.
- **Washing too rarely or too often:** both can upset the balance of the scalp.
- **Heavy oiling:** oil left on the scalp for long periods can make some types of flaking worse.
- **Hard water:** minerals can leave the scalp feeling dry and coated. See our guide to [hard water hair damage](/blog/hard-water-hair-damage-bangalore).
- **Stress and weather changes:** many people notice flaking gets worse during stressful periods or seasonal changes.

Persistent or severe dandruff can have medical causes. If it doesn't improve with good care, consult a dermatologist.

## A Simple Home Routine That Helps

You don't need a complicated routine. Consistency matters most.

### Wash regularly and gently

- Wash often enough to keep your scalp clean; for many people with dandruff, that means more often, not less
- Use lukewarm water, not hot
- Massage your scalp with your fingertips, not your nails

### Rinse thoroughly

Leftover shampoo and conditioner can sit on the scalp and add to flaking. Rinse until your hair feels completely clean.

### Keep conditioner on the lengths

Apply conditioner to the lengths and ends, not the scalp.

### Go easy on heavy oil

If you oil your hair, keep it light and wash it out thoroughly. Ask your stylist or a dermatologist what suits your scalp.

### Don't scratch

Scratching irritates the scalp and can make it worse. Gently massage instead.

### Clean your combs and pillowcases

Wash combs and brushes regularly and change pillowcases often.

### Consider an anti-dandruff shampoo

Many people find a gentle anti-dandruff shampoo helps. Follow the instructions on the pack. If your scalp feels irritated, stop and consult a dermatologist.

## Dandruff and Hair Fall

Many people worry that dandruff causes hair fall. Scratching an itchy scalp can lead to some hair breaking, and an irritated scalp can feel uncomfortable. But hair fall can have many causes, including stress, diet, hormones and health conditions.

An Anti-Hairfall Hair Spa nourishes the roots and helps reduce breakage-related hair fall. If you notice thinning or hair fall that continues, consult a dermatologist.

## Seasonal Dandruff in Bangalore

Many people notice their scalp changes with the seasons:

- **Monsoon:** humidity and damp hair that stays wet for hours can leave the scalp feeling oily and itchy. Dry your hair properly after getting caught in the rain.
- **Winter:** cooler, drier air can leave the scalp tight and flaky. Avoid very hot water.
- **Summer:** sweat and dust can build up. Wash regularly and rinse well after workouts.

## A Simple Weekly Scalp Routine

Here's an easy routine that works for many people with a flaky scalp. Adjust it to your own scalp and your stylist's advice.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Every wash | Gentle shampoo on the scalp, lukewarm water, thorough rinse |
| Every wash | Conditioner on the lengths only |
| After the rain or a workout | Wash or rinse, and dry your hair properly |
| Every week | Wash combs and brushes; change your pillowcase |

## Questions to Ask at Your Scalp Consultation

Your stylist will check your scalp before any spa. It helps to come ready with a few details:

- How often you wash your hair, and with what
- Whether you oil your hair, and how long you leave it on
- When the flaking started, and whether it changes with the seasons
- Whether your scalp itches, feels sore or looks red
- Any products or treatments you've tried

This helps your stylist recommend the right spa, and spot signs that you should see a dermatologist.

## When to See a Dermatologist

Salon treatments and home care help many people, but they're not a substitute for medical advice. See a dermatologist if:

- Flaking continues despite good care
- Your scalp is red, sore, swollen or very itchy
- You notice patches, scaling or crusting
- You have hair loss or thinning
- The flaking spreads to your face, eyebrows or body

If the issue persists, consult a dermatologist.

## Common Mistakes

- **Washing less often to "rest" the scalp,** which can let oil and flakes build up
- **Using very hot water**
- **Leaving heavy oil on the scalp overnight, every night**
- **Scratching**
- **Switching products every few days** without giving any routine time to work
