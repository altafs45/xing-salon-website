School mornings are busy enough without wrestling with tangled hair or a fringe in your child's eyes. A good **kids haircut** is neat, comfortable and easy to manage, so getting ready takes minutes, not a battle. This guide covers how often children need a trim, easy styles that work for school, how to handle tricky hair and how to make salon visits stress-free.

## How Often Do Kids Need a Haircut?

A trim every **6–8 weeks** keeps the shape neat for most children. You may need to book sooner if:

- Your child has a **short cut** that loses shape quickly
- They have a **fringe** that grows into their eyes
- Their hair **grows fast**
- **School rules** require a particular length

Signs it's time for a trim:

- Hair in their eyes
- Hair touching the collar, if school rules require shorter hair
- Tangles that are hard to brush out
- A shape that looks uneven or untidy

## Easy School Haircuts for Boys

Boys' haircuts should be neat for school and easy to manage at home.

- **Classic crop:** short, tidy and very low-maintenance
- **Short back and sides:** a timeless, school-friendly cut
- **Gentle fade:** a neat, modern look kept soft for younger boys
- **Slightly longer on top:** can be combed neatly for school and worn more casually at weekends

At Xing, our **Boys Haircut** is a neat, comfortable cut for school and play, from classic crops to gentle fades.

## Easy School Haircuts for Girls

Girls' cuts should look lovely and be easy to manage every day.

- **Simple trim:** keeps long hair healthy and tidy
- **Bob:** neat, practical and quick to style
- **Fringe:** looks sweet, but needs regular touch-ups
- **Long with layers:** easier to brush and tie up for school

At Xing, our **Girls Haircut** includes trims, bobs and fringes that look lovely and are easy to manage every day.

### Tip for long hair

If your daughter has long hair, a style that ties up easily for school, sports and hot days is practical. Regular trims stop split ends and tangles.

## Choosing a Low-Maintenance Cut

Ask yourself:

- **How long can we spend on hair each morning?**
- **Does my child like having their hair brushed?**
- **Will it need to be tied up for school or sports?**
- **Are there school rules about length?**

Share these with your stylist, and they'll suggest a cut that suits your child's hair and your routine.

## Handling Tricky Hair

### Cowlicks

A cowlick is a section of hair that grows in a different direction. Tell your stylist, and they'll cut to work with it, not against it.

### Curly hair

Curly hair shrinks as it dries and can be prone to tangles. A stylist will cut with this in mind, and regular trims help curls keep a good shape. Detangle gently with conditioner and a wide-tooth comb.

### Thick hair

Thick hair can feel heavy and hot. A stylist can remove some bulk so it's cooler and easier to manage.

### Fine hair

Fine hair can look flat. Shorter, blunt cuts often make it look fuller.

## Kids Hair Care at Home

- **Brush gently** from the ends up, especially with long or curly hair
- **Use a mild, child-friendly shampoo**
- **Use conditioner** if hair tangles easily
- **Tie long hair loosely** for school, with soft ties
- **Dry hair properly** after swimming or getting caught in the rain

## Before School Starts

Book a trim a week or two before the new school year or term. That way, hair looks neat for the first day, and you avoid the rush of last-minute appointments.

## Common Kids Haircut Mistakes

- **Waiting until hair is in their eyes**
- **Choosing a style that needs daily styling** on busy mornings
- **Cutting fringes at home**, which often ends up uneven
- **Booking during nap time** or when your child is hungry

## Haircuts for Different Ages

### Toddlers (1–3 years)

At this age, the goal is simply tidy and comfortable. Short sessions work best. A trim to keep hair out of the eyes and neaten the back is usually enough. Many toddlers sit on a parent's lap.

### Preschoolers (3–5 years)

Children start to have opinions about their hair. Involve them in small choices, like whether they want their fringe shorter, so they feel part of it.

### School-age children (5–10 years)

Practicality matters most. Choose cuts that work with school rules, sport and swimming. Older children may want a particular style; your stylist can suggest a version that's easy to manage.

## Swimming, Sport and Hot Weather

Active children need hair that copes with sport and sweat:

- **Shorter cuts** dry faster after swimming and stay cooler in the heat
- **Long hair** should be tied up for sport and rinsed after swimming
- **Rinse hair with clean water** after swimming in chlorinated pools, and use conditioner if it feels dry
- **A trim before summer holidays** keeps hair manageable through a season of play

## Keeping Kids Comfortable in the Chair

Children who feel relaxed sit more still, and haircuts go faster. A few simple ideas:

- Let your child watch in the mirror, so nothing feels like a surprise
- Explain each step in a friendly way, like "now we're tidying the back"
- Bring a small toy to hold
- Offer praise after, not bribes during

If your child is anxious about clippers or water sprays, tell your stylist. Scissors-only cuts or a damp comb can often be used instead.
