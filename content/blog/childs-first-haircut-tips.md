Your child's first haircut is a small milestone, and for many families, a big moment. It can also be a little nerve-wracking, for your child and for you. With a bit of preparation, your **baby's first haircut** can be calm, quick and even fun. This guide covers when to book, how to prepare, what to bring and how to keep things relaxed on the day.

## When Should Your Baby Get Their First Haircut?

There's no perfect age. Every child's hair grows differently. Many parents book a first haircut when:

- **Hair gets in their eyes**
- **Hair looks uneven** or grows in different lengths
- **Hair becomes tangled** and hard to manage
- **It's simply time** for a tidier look

Some families also have cultural or religious traditions around a child's first haircut. Do what feels right for your family.

## Choosing the Right Time of Day

Timing makes a big difference:

- **Pick a time when your child is rested.** Avoid nap time.
- **Make sure they've eaten.** Hungry children get restless quickly.
- **Avoid busy, rushed days** if you can.
- **Mornings often work well** for younger children.

## Preparing Your Child

### Talk about it

A few days before, talk about the haircut in a calm, positive way. Explain that the stylist will help make their hair neat, and that you'll be right there.

### Play pretend

Some families "practise" at home: pretending to cut a toy's hair, or gently combing and spraying a little water on your child's hair.

### Read or watch something about haircuts

A simple picture book or video about getting a haircut helps some children know what to expect.

### Keep it positive

Avoid words that might sound scary. Focus on how nice their hair will look and how proud you'll be.

## What to Bring

- **A favourite toy or book** for comfort
- **A snack** for after (check with the salon about eating in the chair)
- **A spare top,** in case of itchy hair clippings
- **A reference photo,** if you have a look in mind
- **A small bag or envelope** if you'd like to keep a lock of hair as a keepsake

## Telling Your Stylist

When you book, mention that it's your child's first haircut. At Xing, we'll keep things slow and gentle.

At the appointment, tell your stylist:

- The look you want, or show a reference photo
- About any cowlicks or tricky areas
- Anything your child dislikes, like water sprays or the sound of clippers
- If your child is sensitive to noise or touch

## What to Expect at the Salon

At Xing, a kids haircut usually goes like this:

2. **Get comfortable:** your child settles into the chair, with you close by

## Keeping Your Child Calm in the Chair

- **Stay close** and keep your voice calm and cheerful
- **Hold their hand** or let them sit on your lap
- **Use their toy or book** as a distraction
- **Praise them** for sitting still, even for short moments
- **Don't worry about perfection.** A few wriggles are normal, and our stylists are used to it

### If your child gets upset

It happens, and it's okay. Our stylists will pause, let your child settle and try again. If your child isn't ready, it's fine to finish another day. A short, calm first haircut is better than a long, stressful one.

## A Keepsake

Many parents like to keep a lock of hair from the first haircut. Just ask your stylist before they start, and bring a small bag or envelope.

## After the First Haircut

- **Celebrate** with a small treat or a photo
- **Wash your child's hair** at home to remove any itchy clippings
- **Talk about how well they did**, so the next visit feels easier

## Common First Haircut Mistakes

- **Booking during nap time**
- **Arriving hungry or rushed**
- **Building it up as a big, scary event**
- **Expecting a perfect, still child**
- **Forgetting a comfort toy**

## A First Haircut Checklist

**Before the day**

- Choose a time after a nap and a meal
- Tell your child about the haircut in a calm, positive way
- Mention it's a first haircut when you book

**Bring**

- A favourite toy or book
- A spare top
- A reference photo, if you have one
- A small envelope for a keepsake lock of hair

**On the day**

- Arrive a few minutes early, so nothing feels rushed
- Stay close, or hold your child on your lap
- Keep your voice cheerful and relaxed
- Praise your child afterwards

## Clippers or Scissors?

Many young children are nervous about the buzzing sound of clippers. For a first haircut, stylists often use scissors only, which are quieter and gentler. If your child is curious about clippers, your stylist can let them hear the sound first or touch the clippers while they're switched off. Tell your stylist which you'd prefer when you arrive.

## What If Your Child Has Very Fine or Sparse Hair?

Many babies and toddlers have fine, soft or uneven hair. A first haircut doesn't need to be big. Often, a gentle tidy around the ears and neck and a light trim of the longest pieces is all that's needed. Your stylist will suggest the least they need to do.

## Siblings and First Haircuts

If an older sibling is having a haircut, let your little one watch first. Seeing a brother or sister happily sit in the chair can make the whole experience feel familiar and safe. Many families book siblings back to back for this reason.

## Making the Next Visit Easier

The first haircut sets the tone for future visits. Keep the memory positive: talk about how well your child did, show them photos, and book the next trim before hair gets too long, so the next visit is quick and easy.

## Choosing a Child-Friendly Salon

Look for a salon where staff are patient with children, where you can stay close by, and where the stylist is happy to pause when your child needs a break.
