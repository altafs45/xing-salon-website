It's one of the most common questions we hear: **how often should you get a haircut**? The honest answer depends on your hair length, your style and how you treat your hair. A short bob and long, one-length hair need very different schedules. This guide explains how often to trim by length and style, the signs it's time, and why regular trims actually help you grow your hair.

## The Short Answer

Most women book a trim every **6–10 weeks** to keep the ends healthy and the shape intact. Where you fall in that range depends on:

- **Your length and style**
- **How much heat you use**
- **Whether your hair is coloured or lightened**
- **How quickly your ends split**

## How Often Should You Get a Haircut? By Length

### Short hair and pixie cuts

Short cuts lose their shape quickly, because even a little growth changes the look. Book at the shorter end of the range, or sooner if your style is very precise.

### Bobs and lobs

Bobs look best when the line is sharp. As they grow, they can start to look heavy or uneven. Regular trims keep the shape crisp.

### Shoulder-length hair

Shoulder-length hair can flip out awkwardly as it grows and rubs on your shoulders. Trims every 6–10 weeks keep it neat.

### Long hair

Long, one-length hair can sometimes go a little longer between trims, especially if it's healthy. But long hair is also older at the ends, so it's more prone to split ends. Light trims keep it looking thick and healthy.

### Waist-length hair

Very long hair has the oldest ends of all. Regular light trims protect your length by stopping splits travelling upwards.

## How Often by Style

### Layers

Layers lose their shape as they grow, especially shorter ones. Face-framing layers may need touch-ups more often. Read our [layered haircut guide](/blog/layered-haircut-guide) for more.

### Bangs and fringes

Bangs grow into your eyes quickly. Most people need a quick bang touch-up more often than a full haircut.

### Blunt cuts

A blunt line shows growth and splits clearly. Regular trims keep it sharp.

### Curly hair

Curls hide uneven ends, but they're also prone to dryness and splits. Trims help curls hold a good shape.

## Signs You Need a Haircut

Don't just count weeks. Watch for:

- **Split ends:** frayed or forked tips
- **Tangling** at the ends
- **Thin, see-through ends** compared with the rest of your hair
- **A lost shape:** your cut looks shapeless or heavy
- **Flat or heavy hair** that won't hold volume
- **Styling takes longer** than it used to
- **Bangs in your eyes**

If you notice several of these, book a trim. Our guide to [split ends treatment](/blog/split-ends-causes-treatment) explains why trims are the only real fix for split ends.

## Factors That Mean More Frequent Trims

- **Daily heat styling:** blow drying, ironing and curling wear ends faster
- **Colour and lightening:** make hair more fragile
- **Dryness and hard water:** lead to brittle ends
- **Tight hairstyles:** cause breakage
- **Rough handling:** brushing wet hair roughly, rubbing with towels

If any of these apply, aim for the shorter end of the 6–10 week range.

## Growing Your Hair? Keep Trimming

It sounds backwards, but regular trims help you grow longer hair. Split ends travel up the strand and cause breakage. If you skip trims, your hair breaks as fast as it grows, and the ends look thin and scraggly.

When you're growing your hair:

- **Ask for a light trim** that removes only the damaged ends
- **Tell your stylist** you're growing it, so they take off as little as possible
- **Protect your ends** with heat protectant, gentle detangling and regular conditioning
- **Book a hair spa** to keep lengths soft and strong

## Keeping Your Cut Looking Fresh Between Trims

- Use a heat protectant before blow drying, ironing or curling
- Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase
- Use dry shampoo at the roots to stretch a blow dry by a day or two
- Pair your haircut with a hair spa to keep lengths soft

## A Simple Haircut Calendar

| Your hair | Typical trim rhythm |
| --- | --- |
| Short, pixie or precise bob | Shorter end of 6–10 weeks, or sooner |
| Shoulder-length or layered | Every 6–10 weeks |
| Long, one-length and healthy | Around 6–10 weeks; sometimes a little longer |
| Bangs | Quick touch-ups between full cuts |
| Coloured or heat-styled | Shorter end of the range |

Your stylist can suggest a rhythm that suits your hair.

## Common Mistakes

- **Waiting until your hair looks damaged** to book
- **Skipping trims while growing your hair**
- **Cutting your own bangs or layers** without practice
- **Not telling your stylist** you want to keep length

## Haircuts and Hair Health

Regular haircuts are about more than shape. They're one of the simplest ways to keep hair healthy:

- **Removing split ends** stops breakage travelling up the hair
- **Thick, even ends** make hair look fuller and longer
- **A good shape** makes daily styling quicker, so you use less heat
- **A trim is a good moment to check** your hair's condition and book a spa if it needs one

## Planning Haircuts Around Events

If you have a wedding, party or photoshoot coming up:

- **Book your haircut one to two weeks before** if you're trying a new style, so it has time to settle
- **Book a light trim a few days before** if you're keeping your style and just want it fresh
- **Pair your trim with a blow dry** or styling on the day of the event

## Haircuts for Coloured Hair

Coloured and lightened hair is more fragile, so the ends wear faster. Many people book a trim alongside their root touch-up or colour refresh, so both stay fresh. Ask your stylist whether to trim before or after colouring on the day.

## Questions to Ask Your Stylist

- How often should I come back for this style?
- How much should I take off to remove the split ends?
- Can you keep my length while I grow it?
- Do my bangs or layers need separate touch-ups?

## Making Haircuts Part of Your Routine

The easiest way to stay on schedule is to book your next trim before you leave the salon. Pick a date 6–10 weeks ahead, or sooner for short styles and bangs, and you'll never end up with overgrown, split ends again. Many clients pair their trim with a hair spa every other visit to keep lengths soft and strong.
