You brush your hair, look closely at the ends and see them: tiny forks, frayed tips and strands that look thinner at the bottom than the top. Split ends are one of the most common hair concerns, and one of the most misunderstood. Many products promise to repair them, but the honest answer about **split ends treatment** is simple: a trim removes them, and good habits stop them coming back.

This guide explains what causes split ends, why a trim is the only real fix, how to prevent new ones and how salon treatments help keep your lengths healthy.

## What Is a Split End?

Each strand of hair has a protective outer layer. Over time, that layer at the tip of the hair wears away. Once it's gone, the inner part of the strand starts to fray and split, usually into two or more ends.

Split ends often look like:

- A strand that divides into a "Y" or several frayed tips
- Ends that look thinner, lighter or more see-through
- White dots along the hair, where the strand is weak
- Ends that tangle easily and feel rough

## What Causes Split Ends?

Split ends develop from everyday wear and tear. The most common causes are:

### Heat styling

Daily blow drying, ironing and curling dry out the hair and weaken the outer layer. Our guide to [heat styling without the damage](/blog/heat-styling-hair-damage) explains how to protect your hair.

### Colouring and lightening

Colour, highlights and especially lightening make hair more porous and fragile, so the ends wear faster.

### Rough handling

- Brushing wet hair roughly
- Rubbing hair dry with a towel
- Tight hairstyles and elastic bands
- Sleeping on rough pillowcases

### Dryness

Dry hair is brittle and breaks more easily. Hard water, sun and harsh shampoos all make it worse.

### Time between trims

The ends are the oldest part of your hair. The longer you go without a trim, the more wear they've taken.

## Why a Trim Is the Only Real Split Ends Treatment

This is the part many people don't want to hear. Once a hair has split, it can't be permanently glued back together.

Products like serums and leave-in conditioners can:

- Smooth the surface so splits look less obvious
- Reduce tangling and frizz
- Protect ends from further damage

But they can't repair the split for good. If split ends aren't trimmed, they can travel further up the hair, leading to more breakage and thinner-looking ends.

**A trim removes the damage cleanly**, so your hair looks thicker and healthier at the ends.

## How Much Will a Trim Take Off?

Many people avoid trims because they're worried about losing length. A light trim, sometimes called a "dusting", removes only the damaged ends. Your stylist will:

- Check how far up the splits go
- Take off as little as possible while removing the damage
- Keep your shape and length where possible

If your splits are severe, your stylist may recommend taking off a little more once, then keeping up regular trims so you can grow your hair healthily from there.

## How Often Should You Trim?

Most women book a trim every **6–10 weeks** to keep the ends healthy and the shape intact. You may need trims at the shorter end of that range if:

- You heat-style often
- Your hair is coloured or lightened
- Your ends split quickly
- You have a short or layered style that needs regular shaping

## How to Prevent Split Ends

### Protect your hair from heat

- Use a heat protectant every time
- Keep tools on a moderate setting
- Give your hair heat-free days

### Handle hair gently

- Detangle with a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends
- Squeeze hair dry with a soft towel instead of rubbing
- Use soft, fabric ties instead of tight elastics
- Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase

### Keep hair moisturised

- Use a gentle, sulphate-free shampoo
- Condition the lengths every time you wash
- Use a weekly mask
- Apply a light serum or leave-in to the ends

### Protect from sun, chlorine and hard water

- Cover your hair in strong sun
- Rinse after swimming
- Consider a shower filter if your water is hard

## Split Ends Treatment Plan: Trim, Treat, Protect

The most effective plan has three parts:

1. **Trim:** remove existing split ends
2. **Treat:** a hair spa, and Olaplex if your hair is coloured, to strengthen and nourish
3. **Protect:** heat protection, gentle handling and regular trims to prevent new splits

## Split Ends and Growing Your Hair Long

It sounds backwards, but regular trims help you grow longer, healthier-looking hair. Split ends travel up the hair and cause breakage, which makes your hair look thinner and stops it looking longer. Removing them regularly keeps your ends thick and strong as your hair grows.

## Common Myths About Split Ends

**"Trimming makes hair grow faster."** Hair grows from the scalp, not the ends. Trims don't change growth speed, but they prevent breakage, so your hair keeps its length.

**"A serum can repair split ends."** It can make them look smoother, but it can't repair them permanently.

**"Only long hair gets split ends."** Any length can get them, especially with heat styling and colouring.

## When to Book a Trim

Book a trim if you notice:

- Frayed or forked ends
- Ends that look thin or see-through
- Hair that tangles easily at the bottom
- Ends that feel rough even after conditioning
- It's been more than 6–10 weeks since your last cut

## Split Ends on Coloured Hair

Coloured and lightened hair is more fragile, so it tends to split sooner. If you colour your hair:

- Book trims at the shorter end of the 6–10 week range
- Add Olaplex to your colour service or spa
- Use a colour-safe, sulphate-free shampoo
- Keep heat styling moderate
