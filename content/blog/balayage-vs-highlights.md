If you want to add brightness and dimension to your hair, you'll quickly face a choice: balayage or highlights? Both lighten parts of your hair, and both can look beautiful. But the technique, the result and the upkeep are different. Understanding **balayage vs highlights** helps you pick the look that suits your hair, your style and how often you want to visit the salon.

This guide compares the two side by side, explains who each one suits and covers how to care for your colour afterwards.

## The Quick Answer

- **Choose highlights** if you want brighter, more defined strands of colour and don't mind refreshing them regularly.
- **Choose balayage** if you want a soft, blended, sun-kissed look that grows out gently and needs fewer visits.

## What Are Highlights?

Highlights are lighter strands woven through your hair. They're placed in a pattern to add brightness, dimension and movement. Because they're placed from close to the root, the effect is more uniform and defined.

There are different ways to have highlights:

- **Full highlights:** lighter strands throughout your hair for overall brightness and dimension.
- **Highlights per foil:** a few targeted highlights placed exactly where you want them, such as face-framing pieces or subtle pops of colour.

**Highlights suit you if:**

- You want a noticeably brighter look
- You like defined, even dimension
- You're happy to refresh your colour regularly

## What Is Balayage?

Balayage is a hand-painted technique. Instead of placing strands in a set pattern, your stylist paints colour freehand onto the hair, usually lighter towards the ends. The result blends naturally from root to tip for a soft, lived-in look.

At Xing, balayage is designed for you after a consultation, so placement, tone and brightness suit your face, your base colour and the way you wear your hair.

**Balayage suits you if:**

- You want a natural, sun-kissed look
- You'd prefer fewer salon visits
- You want colour that grows out without a harsh line

## Balayage vs Highlights: Side by Side

| | Highlights | Balayage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technique** | Strands placed in a pattern | Hand-painted freehand |
| **Look** | Brighter, defined strands | Soft, blended, natural |
| **Best for** | Adding dimension and brightness | Low-maintenance, lived-in colour |
| **Grow-out** | Gradual | The softest, most blended grow-out |
| **Upkeep** | Regular refresh | Fewer visits |

### How to decide in one minute

Ask yourself:

1. **Do I want a bold change or a natural one?** Bold and bright points to highlights. Natural and soft points to balayage.
2. **How often do I want to visit the salon?** If less often, balayage is usually easier to live with.
3. **Where do I want the brightness?** Around the face only? Consider highlights per foil. Through the lengths and ends? Balayage.

## Balayage and Highlights on Indian Hair

Most Indian hair has a dark natural base, and both techniques work beautifully on it. A few things to know:

- **Warm tones** such as caramel, honey, copper and chocolate often look especially natural on dark hair.
- **Pre-lightening** may be needed for lighter or brighter shades, especially if your hair is very dark or previously coloured. Your stylist will confirm this at your consultation.
- **Skin tone matters.** Warm undertones often glow with caramel and honey; cool undertones can suit ash and mocha shades. Our guide on [choosing hair colour for Indian skin tones](/blog/hair-colour-indian-skin-tone) explains more.

## Questions to Ask at Your Colour Consultation

A good consultation makes all the difference. Bring these questions:

- **Will I need pre-lightening for this shade?** It affects how your hair feels and how long the appointment takes.
- **Which technique suits my base and cut?** Your stylist may suggest balayage, highlights or a mix.
- **How will it grow out?** Ask what your hair will look like in a couple of months.
- **How often will I need to come back?** Balayage usually means fewer visits; highlights need a more regular refresh.
- **Should I add Olaplex?** For any service that involves lightening, it's worth asking.
- **What should I use at home?** A colour-safe routine protects your investment.

## Choosing a Shade That Flatters You

Brightness and tone matter as much as technique:

- **Subtle and natural:** soft caramel or honey balayage on a dark base.
- **Brighter and bolder:** fuller highlights or a lighter balayage, possibly with pre-lightening.
- **Face-framing only:** highlights per foil placed around the face for brightness without full commitment.
- **Something different:** a peek-a-boo strand or a few fashion-colour pieces.

## What About Global Colour?

If you want one even shade from roots to ends, for example to cover greys or make a complete colour change, global colour is the option. It's different from both balayage and highlights:

| | Global Colour | Highlights | Balayage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Look** | One even shade | Brighter, defined strands | Soft, blended, natural |
| **Grow-out** | Visible root line | Gradual | Softest, most blended |
| **Maintenance** | Root touch-ups | Regular refresh | Fewer visits |

Some people combine techniques, such as a global colour base with a few face-framing highlights. Your stylist can suggest what suits you.

## Protecting Your Hair During Lightening

Both balayage and highlights involve lightening, which can weaken hair. Two things help:

- **Olaplex:** a bond-building treatment that helps protect and strengthen hair during and after colouring. At Xing, you can add an Olaplex top-up to any colour service. Read more in [what is Olaplex](/blog/what-is-olaplex).
- **Regular hair spas:** a [hair spa](/services/hair-spa) every few weeks keeps lightened hair soft and nourished.

## Aftercare for Balayage and Highlights

To keep your colour looking fresh:

- Wait at least a couple of days before your first wash
- Use a colour-safe, sulphate-free shampoo
- Wash with cool or lukewarm water to reduce fading
- Use a heat protectant before blow drying, ironing or curling
- Protect your hair from strong sun and chlorinated water

Our guide on [how to make hair colour last longer](/blog/make-hair-colour-last) has more detail.

## Highlights and Balayage for Men

Highlights are a popular way for men to add texture and dimension, especially on shorter cuts where defined strands show well. Softer, blended colour works too on longer styles. Shorter hair is cut more often, so it's easier to change or remove colour if you try something new.

## Common Questions We Hear

**"Will balayage look natural on short hair?"** It can, though the blended effect shows most on medium and long hair. Your stylist may suggest highlights or a softer placement for short cuts.

**"Can I go from highlights to balayage?"** Usually yes. Your stylist can blend existing highlights into a softer, balayage-style look over one or more visits.

**"Is balayage only for blondes?"** Not at all. Balayage works in any shade family, including rich browns, caramels and reds.
