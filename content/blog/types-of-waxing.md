If you've ever booked a waxing appointment and been asked "which wax?", you're not alone in wondering what the difference is. Salons usually offer more than one type, and each works best on certain areas and skin types. Understanding the **types of waxing** helps you choose the most comfortable option for each part of your body.

This guide compares flavored wax, cartridge (roll-on) wax and stripless wax: how each works, which areas suit each one, and which is best for sensitive skin.

## The Three Main Types of Waxing

At Xing Premium Unisex Salon, our [waxing services](/services/waxing) use three types of wax:

1. **Flavored wax**
2. **Cartridge (roll-on) wax**
3. **Stripless wax**

All three remove hair from the root, so skin stays smooth for longer than shaving. The difference lies in how they're applied and removed, and which areas they suit best.

## Flavored Wax

**How it works:** the wax is spread onto the skin with a spatula, then removed with strips.

**Best for:** arms, legs and body.

**What it feels like:** classic waxing. It's the method most people picture when they think of waxing.

Flavored wax is a reliable choice for larger body areas like full arms, half legs, full legs, front or back, and stomach.

## Cartridge (Roll-On) Wax

**How it works:** the wax is rolled on evenly from a cartridge, then removed with strips.

**Best for:** larger areas, where quick and even application helps.

**What it feels like:** smooth, even coverage.

Because the wax goes on in a thin, even layer, cartridge wax is efficient for larger areas like arms and legs. It's a good choice if you want a quick session on big areas.

## Stripless Wax

**How it works:** the wax is applied and removed without strips. It sets on the skin and is lifted off on its own.

**Best for:** the face, underarms and bikini line, and other delicate areas.

**What it feels like:** often gentler on sensitive areas.

Stripless wax is the go-to for smaller, more delicate areas: upper lip, lower lip, chin, forehead, side locks, full face, underarms and bikini.

## Which Wax Is Best for Sensitive Skin?

If your skin is sensitive, stripless wax is often preferred for delicate areas like the face, underarms and bikini line. A few more tips:

- **Tell your therapist** about sensitive skin, a skin condition, recent sunburn or any skin medication before you start.
- **Avoid exfoliating, sunbathing and retinoid or acne creams** on the area for a few days before waxing.
- **Skip lotions and oils** on the day of your appointment, so the wax grips properly.
- **Follow aftercare carefully** to keep skin comfortable.

If you're pregnant, let your therapist know beforehand.

## Getting the Best Results From Any Wax

### Let your hair grow to the right length

Around the length of a grain of rice works best, so the wax can grip the hair properly. Too short and the wax can't grip; too long and it can be less comfortable.

### Prep your skin

- Avoid scrubbing, sunbathing and retinoid or acne creams on the area for a few days
- Skip lotions and oils on the day

### Follow aftercare

- Avoid sun exposure, hot showers, saunas and heavy workouts for 24 hours
- Wear loose clothing over waxed areas
- Gently exfoliate after two to three days to help prevent ingrown hairs

## Common Waxing Myths

**"Waxing makes hair grow back thicker."** Waxing removes hair from the root. With regular waxing, many people find regrowth looks finer over time, though results vary.

**"You should shave between waxing appointments."** Shaving cuts hair at the surface and can make the next wax less effective. If you can, let hair grow to about the length of a grain of rice before your next appointment.

**"Stripless wax doesn't work on body hair."** Stripless wax is often preferred for delicate areas, but your therapist chooses the wax that suits each area and your skin.

**"Waxing is only for women."** Waxing is simply a hair-removal method. Ask your salon which services are available for you.
