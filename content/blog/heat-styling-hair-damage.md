Straighteners, curling tongs and blow dryers make styling quick and easy. Used every day without care, though, they slowly dry hair out until it's rough, frizzy and breaking. The good news is you don't have to give up heat styling. You just need to understand how **heat damage hair** happens, and change a few habits.

This guide explains what heat does to hair, how to use heat protectant properly, safer temperature habits, the signs of damage and the treatments that help.

## How Heat Damages Hair

Healthy hair has a smooth outer layer that holds moisture in and reflects light. High heat does two things:

1. **It dries hair out.** Heat evaporates the moisture inside the hair.
2. **It roughens the outer layer.** Repeated high heat lifts and weakens the surface.

Over time, hair becomes dry, porous and fragile. It loses shine, frizzes easily and breaks. Colouring, bleaching and hard water make it more vulnerable.

## Signs of Heat Damage Hair

Watch for these signs:

- **Dry, rough ends** that feel like straw
- **More split ends** than usual
- **Frizz** that won't settle, even after conditioning
- **Dullness** and lost shine
- **Breakage:** short, broken hairs, especially around the face and crown
- **Tangles** that are hard to brush out
- **Hair that won't hold a style** like it used to

If several sound familiar, it's time to change your routine.

## Heat Protectant: Your First Line of Defence

A heat protectant creates a barrier that helps reduce the effect of heat on your hair. It doesn't make hair heatproof, but used every time, it makes a real difference.

### How to use it properly

- **Apply it every single time** you blow dry, iron or curl
- **Apply to damp hair before blow drying,** and to dry hair before ironing or curling, following the product instructions
- **Cover all sections,** especially the ends
- **Comb it through** so it's evenly spread
- **Let it absorb** before using hot tools

## Safer Temperature Habits

### Use the lowest heat that works

You don't always need the highest setting. Fine or damaged hair usually needs less heat than thick, coarse hair. Start lower and increase only if needed.

### Fewer passes

Passing a straightener over the same section again and again adds damage. Use smaller sections and one or two smooth passes instead.

### Never iron damp hair

Ironing hair that's still damp can damage it badly. Make sure hair is completely dry before using a straightener or curling tongs.

### Keep tools moving

Don't hold a hot tool on one spot for long.

### Keep the nozzle on your dryer

Pointing the dryer down the hair shaft, with the nozzle attached, helps keep the surface smooth and reduces direct heat on one spot.

## Daily Habits That Reduce Heat Damage

- **Give your hair heat-free days.** Let it air-dry when you can.
- **Rough dry first.** Remove most of the moisture gently before using a brush and dryer.
- **Choose heat-free styles** sometimes: braids, buns or natural texture.
- **Use a wide-tooth comb** on wet hair, starting from the ends.
- **Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase** to reduce friction.

## Repair Options for Heat Damaged Hair

### Trims for split ends

Split ends can't be glued back together. The only way to remove them is to cut them off. Regular trims stop splits travelling up the hair. Our guide to [split ends](/blog/split-ends-causes-treatment) explains more.

## A Heat Styling Routine That Protects Your Hair

1. **Wash** with a gentle, sulphate-free shampoo and condition the lengths
2. **Towel dry gently** by squeezing, not rubbing
3. **Apply heat protectant** to damp hair
4. **Rough dry** on a moderate setting
5. **Style** with a brush and dryer, nozzle pointing down
6. **Iron or curl only when hair is fully dry,** with protectant, moderate heat and few passes
7. **Finish** with a light serum on the ends

## Heat Damage and Hair Colour

Coloured and lightened hair is more fragile, and high heat fades colour faster. If your hair is coloured:

- Lower the temperature on your tools
- Always use heat protectant
- Use a colour-safe, sulphate-free shampoo
- Add Olaplex or a nourishing spa to your routine

## Common Myths

**"Higher heat means a longer-lasting style."** Not necessarily. Good technique and the right products matter more.

**"Heat protectant means I can use any temperature."** It reduces damage; it doesn't remove the risk.

**"Damaged hair can be fully repaired."** Split ends need a trim. Treatments help damaged hair feel and behave better, and good habits stop further damage.

## Choosing the Right Tools

The tools you use make a difference too:

- **Choose tools with adjustable temperature,** so you can use only the heat your hair needs
- **Use a dryer with a nozzle attachment** to direct airflow and avoid hot spots
- **Keep plates and barrels clean,** as product build-up can create uneven heat
- **Replace worn tools** with damaged or uneven plates
- **Use the right brush:** a round brush for volume and a paddle brush for smoothing

## Heat-Free Styling Ideas

Give your hair a rest with styles that need no heat:

- **Braids:** a loose braid on damp hair gives soft waves the next day
- **Buns:** a twisted bun keeps hair neat and protected
- **Air-drying:** with a little leave-in conditioner or serum on the ends
- **Low ponytails** with soft, fabric ties

Even two or three heat-free days a week help your hair recover.

## Heat Styling for Fine, Thick and Curly Hair

**Fine hair:** damages easily, so keep heat low and use fewer passes. Lightweight heat protectants won't weigh it down.

**Thick hair:** may need a little more heat to style, so sectioning carefully and using a protectant on every section matters even more.

**Curly or wavy hair:** daily straightening takes a toll. Consider styling with heat less often, or ask your stylist whether a smoothing treatment would cut down how much you need to iron.
